# Real-data-calibrated matches + multi-attribute players — a grounded V2 read

## What the code actually is (so we stop pretending)

Two facts anchor everything. First, the player model is `{ id, name, cat, rating, club }` (`data.ts:53`) and the sim only ever sees `FieldSlot.rating` (`squad.ts:236`). Every behavioural knob in the 673-line engine — `pace = 0.85 + (rating-70)/60` (`match-sim.ts:80`), pass stray-chance, `TACKLE_RATE`, shot accuracy `(rating-55)/45`, foul/tackle probabilities — is a function of that **one scalar**. Second, the *scoreline is not the sim's*: `tournament.ts::simulateMatch` draws goals as `poisson(0.35 + 2.4 * (sh/total))` from XI average rating, and scorers/minutes/cards are pure `Math.random()`. `match-sim.ts` is explicitly cosmetic and scripts a goal into a corner via `scoreFor()`.

That split is the whole ballgame for grading the second AI's proposals.

## Proposal 2 — "multi-attribute players are a drop-in." Half right, and the wrong half is load-bearing.

Swapping `rating: number` for `attrs: { pace, shooting, passing, dribbling, defending, physical, stamina, gk }` is *typologically* trivial — extend the `Player` interface, thread the vector through `FieldSlot`. But calling it a "drop-in" hides the real work: **nothing in the sim consumes those axes yet.** You get zero realism until you rewire ~20 call sites in `match-sim.ts` and the `avgRating` lambda in `tournament.ts` to read the *right* axis: `pace` → the `pace`/`SPRINT` term; `passing` → stray-chance and `acc`; `defending` → `TACKLE_RATE` and press foul rate; `shooting` → shot `acc`; `stamina` → the `stam` fatigue decay (`match-sim.ts:452`); `gk` → keeper save roll. That's a **day of careful, test-guarded editing**, not a drop-in — and it's where the value is, because it's what makes a fast-but-wasteful winger *feel* different from a solid defender instead of both being "82".

The genuinely under-scoped part is **where the numbers come from**. "Derive from FBref per-90 → position-normalized z-scores/PCA" is real methodology (this is essentially how FiveThirtyEight and the analytics community build ratings), but PCA on per-90 stats gives you *latent axes that don't map to your seven named ones* — PC1 is usually "attacking involvement," not "pace." For a subs game you don't want a defensible loadings matrix; you want seven believable numbers per player. **Skip PCA.** Map curated per-90 metrics directly to axes (progressive carries → dribbling, tackles+interceptions per-90 → defending, xG/shot → shooting) and position-normalize with a simple within-`cat` z-score. That's an afternoon of pandas, not a research project. **And critically for THIS product: your players are drafted onto national-team country slots (`COUNTRIES`, `data.ts:35`) with mock names — there is no real player identity to attach FBref data to.** Real per-player attributes only matter if the draft pool becomes real named players. Until then, multi-attribute vectors are best generated *synthetically* (sample each axis from a position-conditioned distribution you fit offline), which sidesteps the entire licensing problem below.

## Proposal 3 — "calibrate the constants from StatsBomb: single highest-value sim upgrade, light." Right idea, wrong grade.

Calibration is the highest-*realism* lever, but "light" is optimistic. The honest split:

**xG model — genuinely light and high-value.** A logistic regression on shot `(distance, angle, is_header, is_open_play)` from StatsBomb open shots is a classic ~40-line statsmodels fit. Export ~6 coefficients as JSON; the TS side evaluates `1/(1+exp(-z))` in three lines. This is the one place the second AI is exactly right. **But note where it plugs in:** right now shots don't decide goals — `tournament.ts` does, via Poisson. An xG model only earns its keep once you take V1's advice and **unify the cosmetic sim and the authoritative scoreline behind one xG/Dixon-Coles model** so on-screen shots and the final score agree. Bolting xG onto the current cosmetic-only sim changes nothing the viewer can verify.

**Pass-completion-vs-distance, tackle-success, foul rates — real but medium, and diminishing.** Fitting `P(complete | distance, pressure)` from StatsBomb events is doable, but the payoff is subtle: viewers won't notice that a 30m pass now completes 61% vs a hand-tuned 65%. This is the classic sim-calibration trap V1 flagged — high effort, low *product* ROI. Do the xG model; treat the rest as a slow backlog.

**The determinism landmine the second AI ignores entirely.** V1's keystone is a deterministic seeded sim with integer authority, precisely because `Math.hypot`/`Math.exp` aren't bit-identical across engines — and this sim is *saturated* with `Math.random()`, `Math.hypot` (`match-sim.ts:72`), and `Math.exp` (friction, `:395`). If "watched together" is ever going to work by broadcasting a seed (V1's near-free live model), calibrated float coefficients make the divergence *worse*, not better. Any calibration work must land **after** the sim is reworked to a seeded PRNG + fixed timestep + integer/fixed-point positions. Calibrating a non-deterministic float sim is polishing a car with no brakes.

## Licensing — the part that's a real trap, stated plainly

- **StatsBomb open data**: free but **non-commercial, attribution-required** (their user agreement). A subs game monetized via channel points is *plausibly commercial*, and the repo is **public = redistribution**. **Safe:** fit models *offline on the GPU box*, commit only **derived coefficients** (a logistic regression's 6 numbers, a distribution's mean/σ) — coefficients are not "the data." **Unsafe:** vendoring StatsBomb JSON, or per-player attribute tables recognizably derived from it, into the public repo.
- **FBref / EA-Sofifa ratings**: **not licensed for redistribution at all.** A committed `players.json` of scraped FIFA ratings is the clearest violation. Use FBref only to *fit distributions offline*; ship synthetic samples.
- **Rule of thumb:** the public repo may contain *model coefficients and synthetic data*; never raw third-party datasets or recognizable per-entity copies of them.

## Ranked steps — realism per honest effort

1. **Rewire the seven-axis vector into the existing sim (synthetic attrs).** *What:* extend `Player`/`FieldSlot`, generate attrs from a position-conditioned distribution (fit offline from FBref *shapes*, ship synthetic), point each sim knob at its axis. *Why:* players finally feel distinct — the single biggest immersion win, and it needs no live data. *Effort:* **M** (~1–2 days, TDD the axis mapping). *Runs:* browser + a one-off GPU/laptop script for the distribution. *Cost:* $0. *Tools:* pandas offline; plain TS. *Trap:* don't PCA; map axes by hand. **← cheapest high-impact first step.**
2. **Seed the RNG + fixed timestep + integer positions in the sim.** *What:* V1's keystone — inject a mulberry32-style PRNG, replace `Math.random()`, quantize positions. *Why:* unlocks free "watched-together" broadcast *and* is the precondition for any calibration to be reproducible. *Effort:* **M–L** (the sim's float saturation makes this real work). *Runs:* browser. *Cost:* $0. *Trap:* half-seeding leaves cross-engine drift.
3. **Offline xG logistic model → JSON coefficients.** *What:* statsmodels logistic on StatsBomb shots (distance/angle/header/open-play); export ~6 floats. *Why:* real shot quality; foundation for unifying sim + scoreline. *Effort:* **S** (an afternoon). *Runs:* GPU box offline (GPU overkill — CPU ms). *Cost:* $0. *License:* ship coefficients only.
4. **Unify cosmetic sim + scoreline behind the xG model.** *What:* let on-screen shots roll against xG and *drive* the score, retiring the separate Poisson in `tournament.ts`. *Why:* play and result finally agree — the thing viewers can actually verify. *Effort:* **M**. *Runs:* browser. *Cost:* $0.
5. **Calibrate pass/tackle/foul distributions from StatsBomb.** *What:* fit completion-vs-distance/pressure, export coefficients. *Why:* distributional realism. *Effort:* **M**, low *visible* payoff. *Runs:* GPU offline → JSON. *Trap:* diminishing returns — backlog it.
6. **Real named-player attribute DB.** *What:* only if the draft pool becomes real players; per-90 → z-scores → JSON. *Effort:* **L** + the licensing minefield. *Verdict:* deferred; the country-slot draft doesn't need it, and player *style embeddings* (proposal 2's latent-vector idea) are a research toy with near-zero product ROI here — skip outright.

**Cheapest high-impact first step:** #1, synthetic seven-axis attrs wired into the existing knobs — pure TS, zero data risk, immediate "these players feel different." **Biggest trap:** calibrating a still-non-deterministic, float-saturated sim (proposal 3 done before #2), which both wastes the effort and actively breaks V1's watched-together path — compounded by vendoring StatsBomb/FBref data into a public, plausibly-commercial repo.
