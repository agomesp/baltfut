# Game design, engagement & the watched-together loop — the long game

## 1. Critique of the v1 + v2 material

**What holds up.** The v1 game-design doc's central diagnosis is correct and load-bearing: *"the gap is entirely the loop — nothing makes a sub come back, and nothing makes a match a shared event"* (`09-game-design.md:1`). Its ranking — persist state first (#1), then the palpites tie-in and the shared clock — is the right spine, because everything social depends on a team surviving a refresh and being *shared*. The observation that believability of the *loop* is a **social** property, not a simulation one (`09:5`, "FM feels real because of the season narrative, not the 2D match engine"), is the single most important framing in either spike and should govern V3: the meta-game, not the pitch fidelity, is what retains subs. The `07-realtime-watched.md` recommendation of **seed-broadcast over per-tick state** (option B, `07:16`) is also correct and is what keeps the watch-party free.

**What holds up but is under-scoped.** Three engagement claims lean on infra costs the game-design doc under-budgets, and the v1 *ROADMAP reality-check* already caught most of them — the game-design doc itself hasn't absorbed those corrections:

- **The palpites tie-in (#4) is sold as "reuse it verbatim."** It is genuinely close — `classifyPrediction`/`rankPredictions` exist (`src/lib/votes/predictions.ts:15,34`) and the server-side cutoff already rejects late palpites. But `classifyPrediction` keys off a *live ESPN scoreline*; subs matches have **no ESPN match**. The cutoff must re-key to the subs match's `kickoff_at`, and the whole thing depends on determinism landing first (below). So it's S-to-wire, M-to-integrate — not verbatim.
- **The shared clock (#2) is the doc's "biggest lever" but its keystone is aimed at the wrong file.** The doc says *"`simulateMatch` already precomputes every MatchEvent with a minute… store the precomputed result plus a kickoff_at"* (`09:13`). Correct in spirit, but the outcome itself is non-deterministic: `tournament.ts` has **11 `Math.random()` sites** (bracket `shuffle`, `poisson`, goal-minute `min()`, `pickWeighted` scorers/cards, `shootout()`, `injuryLength()`), and `match-sim.ts` another 20. The reality-check (`ROADMAP.md:80`, "A2") nails this: **seed only the sim and every viewer still sees a different scoreline, different scorers, and a different bracket.** For a *watched-together* loop the shared thing that matters is the *result and the bracket draw*, which is exactly the half the game-design doc glosses. Determinism of `tournament.ts` is the true precondition for the entire social layer.
- **The `{seed, startEpochMs, goalScript}` payload is internally contradictory** (reality-check C1, `ROADMAP.md:104`): if the seed derives the goals, there is no separate `goalScript` to send. It's an either/or — broadcast **seed-only** and let every client recompute the identical bracket + result. This matters for engagement because "we're watching the same match" must mean the *same scoreline*, not merely synced dots.

**What is missing entirely.** The v1 doc is strong on the *mechanics* of retention (persistence, points, clock) but thin on the **narrative and identity psychology** that actually makes subs tune in for a whole season:

- **No rivalry/relationship graph.** #8 mentions auto-narrative tags ("revenge match", "giant-killer") but there is no persistent head-to-head record between two *subs* (not teams) — the "Gabriel's Brazil vs the mod's Argentina" hook (`09:16`) is asserted but never modelled. A `subs_h2h` record is the cheapest dynasty-building primitive and it's absent.
- **No draft as a live event.** Both spikes treat the *match* as the appointment but ignore that **draft night is the best watch-party of the season** — it's where identity is minted, it's inherently social, and it's snake-draft drama the streamer narrates. It deserves the same synchronized-lobby + Presence treatment as a match.
- **No economy sink / progression ladder.** Palpites points accumulate but there's nothing to *spend* them on. Retention needs a sink (cosmetic kit unlocks, a captain re-pick, a wildcard) or the leaderboard is the only reward and plateaus.
- **No spectator-to-owner funnel design.** The doc correctly warns *"don't hard-gate the funnel behind OAuth"* (`09:16`) but never defines the funnel: what a non-sub sees, what a sub-but-non-owner sees, what an owner sees.

**Where v1 and v2 disagree.** They barely overlap on this theme — v2 is almost silent on the community loop, which is itself telling. v2's contribution is a **warning**: its verdict (c) says a live GPU producer *"detonates Supabase's 2M-message/month quota (~15 msg/s burns the monthly quota in ~36h)"* (`README.md:20`). That directly constrains engagement features — **Presence churn and emote storms are chatty and share that same quota** (reality-check B3, `ROADMAP.md:92`: Presence heartbeats count against messages, and the free project *pauses after 7 days idle* — fatal for an "appointment" product). So the disagreement is implicit: v1's game-design doc treats Realtime as effectively free; v2's cost analysis proves it isn't, once you layer Presence + emotes + live markets on the same channel. V3 resolves it: **broadcast-only ephemera, no persisted emotes, and budget for Supabase Pro the moment the loop proves out.**

## 2. The definitive long-game plan (no time constraints)

The loop, end to end: **Draft night (live event) → group/knockout season with persistent teams → match-day watch-parties with live palpites → standings/awards + shareable clips → carryover (growth, injuries, suspensions, transfers) → rivalries/dynasties → streamer-integrated betting & perks.** Build it in dependency order, not feature-glamour order.

## 3. Ranked steps, every one bucket-tagged

**Rank 1 — Determinism of `tournament.ts` first, then `match-sim.ts` [BUCKET A].** Seed the bracket `shuffle`, `poisson`, goal-minutes, scorer/card `pickWeighted`, `shootout`, `injuryLength` with a `mulberry32` keyed off `bracketSeed` + per-match seeds. TDD: same seed → identical `MatchResult[]` and identical bracket. **Why A:** pure TS, client-only, no infra. **Why it's rank 1:** *every* shared-viewing, watch-party, and live-betting feature is a lie without it — "we watched the same match" must mean the same scoreline.

**Rank 2 — Persist teams/bracket/status to Supabase [BUCKET A].** Tables `subs_teams` (owner identity, roster JSONB), `subs_bracket` (rounds JSONB), `subs_status` (the existing `StatusMap`). Serialize the pure `DraftState` blob per-league; writes only through a `cast-vote`-style Edge Function; reads via `security_invoker` views. **Why A:** Supabase free tier + existing RLS pattern. **Tax the doc ignores** (CLAUDE.md "Grants gotcha"): every new table needs explicit grants to `service_role` + anon **and** `scripts/db/` assertions or CI's `database` job goes red. **Why rank 2:** a team that survives to the quarters is *the* emotional hook (`09:10`) — identity is the retention primitive.

**Rank 3 — Palpites tie-in, re-keyed to subs matches [BUCKET A].** Reuse `classifyPrediction`/`rankPredictions`; open a palpite window on the *fictional* match (scoreline + MVP + pen-winner — the pen-winner feature already ships in prod). Non-owners bet; owners earn bonus when their team wins; resolve against the **authoritative** `tournament.ts` integers, never the cosmetic sim (reality-check trap, `07:52`). **Why A:** the machinery exists; only the cutoff key (subs `kickoff_at`, not ESPN) changes. **Why rank 3:** it fuses two games into one economy — the passive palpites audience now cares about the fantasy tournament. Highest-ROI integration.

**Rank 4 — Synchronized watch-party: seed-broadcast + wall-clock catch-up + Presence [BUCKET A→B boundary].** Broadcast `{bracketSeed, perMatchSeeds, startEpochMs}` (seed-only, per C1); clients derive `currentMinute` from `Date.now()`, fast-forward the deterministic sim, and a backgrounded tab *catches up* instead of freezing. Presence shows "142 subs watching". **Why the A portion works:** if a client is open to broadcast the start, it's free on Supabase. **Why the scheduled kickoff is BUCKET B:** nobody may have a tab open to stamp `startEpochMs` — that needs `pg_cron` + Edge Function to flip status on schedule so the match is a *real event that happens whether or not you're watching*. Presence churn eats the message quota (v2's warning) — keep it broadcast-only and expect Pro ($25) at scale.

**Rank 5 — Draft night as a live synchronized event [BUCKET A, B for scheduling].** Give the snake draft the same lobby-countdown + Presence + shared-clock treatment as a match. **Why A:** same Realtime primitives. **Why B for the scheduled slot:** a fixed draft-night time needs the cron stamp. This is the missing "best watch-party of the season" — cheap, and it mints the identity everything else leans on.

**Rank 6 — Standings, awards & shareable clip cards [BUCKET A].** Golden Boot / Glove / MVP / biggest upset, all pure over stored `MatchEvent[]`; render a static SVG→PNG card (`canvas.toDataURL`) the sub drops in chat. **Why A:** pure computation + client canvas. **Why it ranks here:** shareable "MY team won the cup" is free acquisition — the loop's viral surface — but it's worthless before there's a team to own (ranks 1–2).

**Rank 7 — Rivalry/H2H + narrative layer [BUCKET A].** A `subs_h2h` record between *owners*; auto-tags "revenge match", "giant-killer", "cursed from the spot" from `MatchResult.events`; ticker lines feed the streamer's commentary. **Why A:** pure over stored events. **Why it matters:** narrative is what makes 40 dot-tokens a *story*, and the owner-vs-owner grudge is the retention engine the doc asserts but never builds.

**Rank 8 — Season persistence: growth, transfers, dynasties [BUCKET A].** The carryover engine already exists and is TDD'd — `applyMatchEvents`/`advanceStatus` carry cards, suspensions, and injuries including season-ending `"cup"` (`carryover.test.ts:40`). Extend to a between-season re-draft window, small winner rating-bumps, retained-core rules, a dynasty badge. **Why A:** extends existing pure functions + JSONB. **Trap (from `09:25`):** do NOT build this before ≥1 full tournament has actually been *watched* — premature structure for uncertain payoff. Rank 8, not earlier.

**Rank 9 — Progression sink / economy ladder [BUCKET A].** Spend palpites points on cosmetic kit hue-shifts, a one-time captain re-pick, or a wildcard. **Why A:** DB rows + client render. **Why it's missing and needed:** a leaderboard with no sink plateaus; a sink converts accumulated points into recurring decisions.

**Rank 10 — Twitch/Kick identity + sub-gating [BUCKET B].** OAuth token exchange in a Deno Edge Function; gate *owning a team*, not spectating (`09:16`). **Why B not A:** needs the *broadcaster's* token cached/refreshed hourly (`pg_cron` or a $5 worker) — the first thing that genuinely breaks the "$0" story (reality-check C2). Kick is best-effort; treat Twitch as primary.

**Rank 11 — Channel-points / bits betting + Twitch Predictions bot [BUCKET B].** A persistent EventSub websocket opens a Prediction at kickoff and resolves it from the authoritative scoreline; channel-points buy "next scorer"/live-MVP entries. **Why B:** a persistent bot process needs an always-on worker (Cloudflare/Fly, free–$5). **Quick-win subset that stays A:** live MVP vote is just a Realtime broadcast tally with zero Twitch dependency — ship that first.

**Rank 12 — Live in-match prediction markets [BUCKET B].** "Predict next scorer", "HT result", opened via broadcast `{market_open, closesAt}`, collected through the existing cutoff-enforcing Edge Function, resolved on authoritative outcome. **Why B:** the *value* is A-native (reuses palpites + broadcast), but the **scheduled kickoff that makes markets an appointment** is the B dependency; without it, markets only exist when someone's tab happens to be driving.

**GPU / BUCKET C — explicitly nothing.** No engagement or watched-together feature needs a GPU. Determinism means a ~30-byte seed reconstructs the identical match in every browser for free (v2 verdict c, `README.md:20`); a live GPU producer would *replace* something you already have while detonating the message quota. **State it plainly: the community loop is entirely A + B; C buys it nothing.** The GPU's only defensible role is offline (v2's factory for prediction coefficients), which is a *prediction-accuracy* concern, not an engagement one.

**Sequence:** Rank 1 → 2 → 3 → 4/5 (A parts) → 6 → 7 → [B unlock: scheduled kickoff] → 10 → 11 → 12 → 8 → 9. The one paid dependency worth green-lighting once ranks 1–6 prove the loop holds subs' attention is a single tiny always-on worker + Supabase Pro — it unlocks scheduled kickoffs, Twitch identity, and the betting bot together.
