## Commentary, Audio & Atmosphere for the /subtests Live Match

The sim already emits a clean event stream — `snapshot()` returns an incrementing `eventSeq`, a Portuguese `eventText` ("Escanteio", "⚽ GOL", "🟨 Amarelo", "Defesa"…) and a transient `caption`, stepped by RAF in `pitch-view.tsx`. Every recommendation below hooks that same seam: watch `eventSeq`, fire audio/commentary, tie it to the ticker. Nothing here needs SSR; it's all client-side on the static Pages bundle, with Supabase Storage as the asset CDN and Realtime as the "watch-together" sync bus. That is the single biggest architectural insight: **you don't need a server for immersion, you need the event bus you already have.**

One structural fix must come first regardless of what you build on top.

### Ranked upgrades

**1. Richer event vocabulary from the sim (the multiplier). Effort: S. Client-only.**
Commentary quality is capped by event granularity. Today the sim tickers ~12 event kinds and drops most of the interesting texture (a 30-yard through-ball, a last-ditch block, a keeper's fingertip parry, a shot that rattles the post) into the same generic "Chute"/"Defesa". Before spending on voices, widen the emit surface in `match-sim.ts`: add fields to the snapshot event — `{kind, side, playerId, x, y, danger: 0..1, xg?}` — so downstream commentary/audio can be *contextual* rather than keyword-matched. `danger` is trivially derivable from distance-to-goal + defenders-in-path you already compute in `decide()`. This is the cheapest, highest-leverage change: every later layer (phrase variety, crowd swell, music tension, stat pops) reads from it. WHY it sells realism: real commentary is reactive to *degree* ("and that's… JUST wide!" vs "comfortable for the keeper") — you can't fake that from a flat enum. Trap to avoid: don't let commentary read raw coordinates and re-derive meaning; make the sim the single source of semantic truth, mirroring your existing "one canonical schema" discipline.

**2. Templated phrase-bank commentary (play-by-play). Effort: S–M. Client-only.**
This is the quick win. A pt-BR phrase bank keyed by `event.kind` × `danger` bucket × context (score state, minute, is-it-a-knockout), with 6–12 variants per slot and weighted random selection with a recent-history dedupe so lines don't repeat. This is exactly how *Football Manager*'s text commentary and the classic *Championship Manager* engine work — a few thousand fragments composed with slot-fills (`{scorer}`, `{team}`, `{minute}'`). Structure it as `{lead-in} {action} {colour}`: "Bola trabalhada pela direita… {carrier} arrisca… POR CIMA!" WHY: variety + reactivity is 80% of what makes commentary feel alive, and it's free, offline, zero-latency, and localized. Give the "commentator" a light personality knob (excitable vs dry) to add character. Tie every line into the existing `ticker` state so text and audio stay in lockstep. Trap: a thin bank (2–3 variants) feels *worse* than silence within one match — budget the writing time, and separate **play-by-play** (event-driven) from **colour** (idle filler: "clima tenso aqui na Arena", stat call-outs) so quiet spells aren't dead air. Reference model: EA FC / PES stitch pre-recorded fragments the same way — the algorithm is public even if their audio isn't.

**3. Web Audio SFX layer: whistle, kick, net, post, crowd reactions. Effort: S. Client-only, assets on Supabase Storage.**
A single `AudioContext` with a small buffer pool. Preload ~10 one-shots (kick, whistle short/long, ball-on-post, net ripple, tackle thud, card-brandish sting, "ooh" near-miss, goal roar) as short OGG/MP3 (~200–400 KB total). Fire from the `eventSeq` watcher. Use Web Audio, **not** `new Audio()` — you get polyphony, low latency, per-voice gain, and no GC stalls. WHY: SFX is the single most cost-effective immersion-per-KB win in the whole list; a whistle + net-ripple on a goal does more than any visual. Sources: freesound.org (CC0), Kenney's free game SFX packs. Quick win. Trap: browsers block audio until a user gesture — gate the whole audio system behind the existing "play" button so the `AudioContext` resumes on a real click; and mind that this is a streamer product, so ship a master-mute + volume slider (streamers will overlay their own mic).

**4. Crowd ambience bed that swells with danger. Effort: S–M. Client-only, Storage.**
A looping stadium murmur (~1–2 MB, seamless loop) on its own gain node, whose volume/low-pass tracks a smoothed `danger` from #1: crowd rises as an attack builds, peaks and *roars* on a goal (crossfade to a separate roar stem), drops to a hush on an injury/red card. This "adaptive ambience" is the trick every football broadcast and *FIFA*'s crowd system uses. WHY: a reactive crowd is what makes it feel *watched together* rather than a screensaver — the swell telegraphs "something's coming" before the commentary even speaks. Effort creeps to M only because you want a seamless loop and a good swell curve (a one-pole low-pass on `danger` avoids jitter). Trap: don't drive gain directly off per-frame ball position — it'll chatter; smooth it. Keep total crowd assets small and stream from Storage so they don't bloat the JS bundle or Pages deploy.

**5. TTS commentator voice — Web Speech first, cloud pre-gen later. Effort: S (Web Speech) / M (cloud). Mixed.**
Two tiers. **Tier A (ship first):** browser `speechSynthesis` with a pt-BR voice — free, offline, zero infra, reads the #2 phrase lines aloud. WHY: an actual *voice* crosses the "this is a broadcast" threshold that text alone can't. Trap: voice availability and quality are wildly inconsistent across OS/browser (Chrome-on-Windows pt-BR ≠ Safari-on-iOS), pronunciation of player names is rough, and it can lag under load — so treat it as an enhancement over text, never the sole channel, and cap queue depth (drop stale lines when events pile up, or the voice narrates the past). **Tier B (worth the small budget):** because your phrase bank is a *finite* fragment set, pre-generate every fragment once with a good cloud TTS (ElevenLabs / Azure Neural pt-BR) at build time, store the clips in Supabase Storage, and stitch at runtime like EA does. This gives broadcast-grade voice with **zero per-play cost or latency** and full offline playback — the key realization is that finite templates make cloud TTS a one-time build expense, not a runtime dependency. Trap: **live per-event LLM+TTS is the big trap** — latency (seconds), per-play cost, an API key that can't live in a static bundle (would force an Edge Function on the hot path), and it breaks offline. LLM's only defensible use is *offline*, at build time, to *author more phrase-bank variety*, not to narrate live.

**6. Goal & near-miss "moments": replay flash + stat popup + audio sting. Effort: M. Client-only.**
On a goal (`scoreFor`), you already flash GOAL!. Elevate it into a coordinated moment: freeze/slow the ball trail, punch a full-bleed lower-third ("{scorer} {minute}'"), fire the roar stem, duck the crowd bed under the commentary "GOOOOL!", then a 2-second stat popup (xG this half, possession, shot count — all already in `snapshot()`). Because the sim is deterministic per-goal, a "replay" can just re-run the last ~3s of buffered ball/player positions at 0.5× — no video, pure re-render of stored `Pt[]` frames. WHY: the goal is the payload of the whole watch; a synchronized audio-visual-text hit is what people clip and react to. This is where the layers compound. Trap: don't over-freeze during a live shared watch — keep it under ~4s so latecomers/viewers don't desync.

**7. RAF-freeze fix so audio/commentary survive a background tab. Effort: M. Client-only (+ optional Storage worklet).**
Your MEMORY already documents this: RAF pauses when the tab is hidden, so the live view *and* now all commentary/crowd/SFX freeze — fatal for a streamer whose viewers tab away. Drive the sim clock from a source that survives backgrounding: a Web Worker `setInterval` (workers keep firing) or an `AudioWorklet`/audio-clock tick posting `dt` to the main thread, and render whatever frames you can when visible. The crowd bed and TTS on Web Audio *do* keep playing while hidden (audio isn't throttled the way RAF is), so decoupling the sim step from RAF also keeps the *soundtrack* of the match alive even when pixels are frozen — which is arguably enough for a viewer who's listening. WHY: without this, every audio upgrade above is silenced the moment focus leaves. Note the honest ceiling from your own audit: full window *occlusion* (OBS-covered) still freezes everything — this fix addresses backgrounded-but-visible tabs, which is the common case.

**8. Shared "watch-together" sync via Supabase Realtime. Effort: M. Supabase.**
For a subs audience watching the *same* knockout tie, broadcast the authoritative match clock + goal timestamps over a Realtime channel so everyone's crowd-roar and commentary fire within ~1s of each other (host or a scheduled job is the timekeeper; clients just sync their local sim to the broadcast clock). WHY: a stadium roar is only communal if it lands together; desynced audio across a chat kills the "we all saw that" moment that makes it feel live. Fits free-tier Realtime, no new infra. Trap: don't stream positions (too chatty) — sync only the clock + discrete events and let each client's deterministic sim fill in between, exactly like the goal-replay in #6.

### Bottom line
Sequence it: **#1 → #2 → #3 → #4** is a weekend of work that transforms the feel (semantic events, pt-BR text commentary, SFX, adaptive crowd) with zero infra and zero recurring cost. **#7** must land alongside or the streamer use-case breaks. **#5 Tier A** (Web Speech) is a near-free voice bolt-on; **#5 Tier B** (build-time cloud TTS to Storage) is the one place a few dollars buys broadcast polish without a runtime server. **#6** and **#8** are the "watched together" payoff once the foundation exists. The consistent trap across all of it: anything that puts an LLM or TTS API on the *live per-event* path — it defeats static hosting, offline play, latency, and cost all at once. Keep generation at build time; keep the runtime a finite, deterministic, offline bundle.
