# Statistical realism of outcomes & the prediction mini-game

The believability problem splits cleanly in two: (1) **do the scorelines and bracket outcomes look like real football?** and (2) **is the palpites game fair, well-scored, and calibrated?** Today the subs spike (`src/lib/subs-draft/tournament.ts`) samples two *independent* Poissons with λ tied linearly to XI-average rating, resolves ties with an ad-hoc shootout, and scatters scorers/cards uniformly across 90'. The main app (`src/lib/ai-palpite/predict.ts`) is *fully deterministic* — `1.3 ± gap·0.6`, rounded, no variance — and palpites are scored only as exact/can/losing (`src/lib/votes/predictions.ts`), with **no proper scoring rule and no calibration measurement anywhere**. Everything below runs client-side in plain TS unless noted, which fits the static-Pages + Supabase constraint perfectly — statistical realism is almost entirely a *math* problem, not an infrastructure one.

Two structural facts to anchor on. First, real football scores are famously well-described by a **bivariate Poisson / Dixon-Coles** model — independent Poisson is *close* but systematically wrong on 0-0, 1-0, 0-1, 1-1 (the low-score cells), which is exactly where believability and prediction fairness live. Second, the outcome is *authoritative from the bracket*; the sim is cosmetic. So the highest-leverage work is on the **number-generating model** and the **scoring rules**, not the pitch animation.

## Ranked upgrades

### 1. Dixon-Coles-corrected bivariate scoreline model — QUICK WIN
**S, client-only.** Replace the two independent `poisson()` draws with a Dixon-Coles model. Keep λ_home, λ_away from strength (below), then apply the DC **τ(x,y)** low-score correction and a home-field factor. The correction is four cells only:
`τ = 1−λμρ (0-0), 1+λρ (0-1), 1+μρ (1-0), 1−ρ (1-1), else 1`, with ρ≈ −0.05 to +0.15. This is ~30 lines. **Why:** it fixes the single most-noticed unrealism — too few draws and too-clean 2-1/3-1 games — and makes the distribution of scorelines match reality (Dixon & Coles 1997 is *the* canonical soccer model; it underpins most betting-market baselines). Sample by building the P(x,y) matrix over x,y∈0..6 and drawing from it, or rejection-sample the independent draw with acceptance ∝τ. **Trap to avoid:** don't just add noise to the deterministic `1.3±gap` — that inflates variance without fixing the *shape*. Use the real joint PMF.

### 2. Strength → λ via a proper Poisson-regression form + home advantage — QUICK WIN
**S, client-only.** The current `0.35 + 2.4·(s/total)` linear map is arbitrary. Use the standard multiplicative form: `λ_home = exp(μ + α_home − β_away + γ)`, `λ_away = exp(μ + α_away − β_home)`, where α=attack, β=defence, γ=home edge (~exp(γ)≈1.3, i.e. +30% goals — but **set γ≈0 for neutral World Cup venues**, keep a small edge only for the actual hosts USA/MEX/CAN). Derive α,β from the drafted-squad ratings (upgrade #4). **Why:** the exp() link is what makes rating gaps translate to *believable* goal ratios instead of a capped linear ramp; it's the Maher-1982 / modern Poisson-regression backbone. Total goals per game should land ~2.7 with a heavy tail — validate against that number.

### 3. Elo (or Glicko-2) ratings that UPDATE across the tournament — M, client-only
**M, client-only.** Today `teamPower` (main app) and squad-average (subs) are static. Add World-Football-Elo-style updates so a team that wins convincingly *earns* strength within the run: `R' = R + K·G·(W − We)`, `We = 1/(1+10^(−dr/400))`, K≈40–60 for a cup, **G = goal-difference multiplier** (G=1.5 for +2, `(11+|gd|)/8` beyond). Convert Elo diff → λ ratio for the model above. **Why:** eloratings.net uses exactly this and it's the accepted "true strength" proxy for internationals; it gives narrative (a Cinderella subs-team that keeps winning becomes genuinely dangerous) and makes upsets *earned* rather than pure RNG. Glicko-2 adds a rating-deviation (uncertainty) term — nicer for freshly-drafted squads with no history, but heavier; **Elo is the right effort/realism trade here.** For the main app's real matches you can even seed from **market-implied** strengths (derive λ from published bookmaker odds via the inverse of the DC model) for a well-calibrated baseline for free.

### 4. Derive team strength from drafted player attributes — M, client-only
**M, client-only.** Right now strength = flat mean of ratings (`avgRating`). Make it positional and non-linear: weight by role for attack/defence separately (attackers+AM drive α; defenders+GK drive β), and use a **convex** aggregation (e.g. mean of ratings³ then cube-root, or top-N weighting) so a squad with one 93 star and ten 71s isn't identical to eleven 78s. **Why:** this is what makes *drafting decisions matter* — the whole point of the subs game. It also lets the same engine feed both the scoreline model (α/β → λ) and the shootout. Mirror EA-FC's approach: separate ATT/MID/DEF team ratings, not one number. **Key tradeoff:** keep it monotonic and legible so subs can see *why* their team is strong.

### 5. Monte-Carlo tournament win-probabilities + live win-prob during a match — M, client-only
**M, client-only (optionally cached in Supabase).** Before/at each round, run 10k–50k simulations of the *remaining* bracket using the model above and show each team's **P(champion), P(reach final)**, etc. During a live watched match, show a **win-probability curve** that updates on each goal/red-card (a lightweight in-play model: remaining-goals Poisson with a time-decay + scoreline state). **Why:** this is the immersion multiplier — FiveThirtyEight's soccer coverage and every modern broadcast live win-prob graphic is what makes people feel they're watching something *real and consequential*. It's embarrassingly parallel and cheap client-side; only cache in Supabase if you want everyone to see identical numbers. **Note:** because rAF pauses in background tabs, drive the *sim clock* off `Date.now()` / a Web Worker (the memory notes already prove Workers survive data-throttling) so the match state and win-prob stay correct even when the pitch animation freezes; re-sync the animation on focus.

### 6. A proper shootout & extra-time model — S, client-only
**S, client-only.** The current shootout (first-5 then repeat) can't produce realistic 4-3 / sudden-death drama and lets ties recur oddly. Model it correctly: strict alternating 5 kicks, per-kick conversion ~0.75 baseline ± keeper/taker rating, **early-stopping** once mathematically decided, then true sudden-death. Add a short extra-time λ (≈0.5 goals/side over 30') *before* pens. **Why:** knockouts are watched live — the shootout is the emotional peak and currently the least realistic subsystem.

## The palpites mini-game (fairness & scoring)

The current exact/can/losing model is fine as *live UI* but is **not a scoring rule** — it can't rank a full season fairly and rewards nothing for being close. Design it as tiered points **plus** a proper probabilistic score:

- **Tiered exact-first points** (Kicktipp/Superbru standard, intuitive, S): exact score = **5**, correct goal-difference (non-exact) = **3**, correct outcome only (W/D/L) = **1**, wrong = 0. Add the existing **pen-winner = +0.5**. This is legible and what football-pool players expect.
- **RPS (Ranked Probability Score)** for the *serious* leaderboard and for calibration (M): RPS = `(1/(r−1))·Σ_i (Σ_{j≤i}(p_j − o_j))²` over ordered outcomes {Home, Draw, Away}. RPS is **the** accepted metric for ordered football outcomes (Constantinou & Fenton 2012) precisely because it rewards *closeness* — predicting a draw when Home wins hurts less than predicting Away. Use **Brier** only if you collapse to unordered classes. If you let users submit *probabilities* (not just a scoreline), RPS is strictly proper — they maximise expected score by reporting true beliefs, which kills gaming.
- **Confidence weighting done right:** don't let raw self-confidence multiply points (that's not proper and invites abuse). Either (a) fixed per-match points, or (b) require a probability triplet and score with RPS. A middle ground: a per-round **"banker" pick** that doubles one match — popular, fair, low-abuse.
- **Leaderboard fairness:** rank by **average** score per match played (not total) so latecomers aren't buried, show it alongside a "vs. the AI model" benchmark (your DC model *is* the house line — beating it is the flex), and keep the existing server-side cutoff (memory: `server-side-cutoff`) so nobody palpita after kickoff. Store scores computed client-side from `vote_entries` + final scores; **no server needed**, though a nightly Supabase Edge Function that materialises a `leaderboard` table would cut client compute and unify numbers.

## Validation — the part nobody skips but everyone forgets
Ship a **calibration harness** (S–M, offline script in `experiments/`): backtest the model against real completed World Cup / qualifier results and plot a **reliability curve** (predicted P(win) bucketed vs. observed frequency — a 45° line = calibrated), plus aggregate **Brier** and **RPS** vs. a naive baseline (always home, or bookmaker line). Tune ρ (Dixon-Coles), K (Elo), and γ (home) to minimise RPS. **Why this is non-negotiable:** "believable outcomes" is a *measurable* claim — without the reliability curve you're guessing whether upsets happen at the right rate. This is exactly how 538 and every serious model reports itself.

## Traps
- **Adding Gaussian noise to the deterministic predictor** — fixes nothing, breaks calibration. Use the joint PMF.
- **Confidence-as-multiplier scoring** — not a proper rule; invites always-max-confidence spam. Use RPS or fixed tiers.
- **Big home advantage at neutral WC venues** — over-tilts every tie. γ≈0 except real hosts.
- **Driving match/sim state off requestAnimationFrame** — freezes in background tabs (known limitation). Keep the *statistical* clock on Date.now()/Worker; let only the cosmetic animation ride rAF.
- **A heavyweight rating system (full Glicko-2 with histories)** before Elo is even in — premature. Elo first, measure, then decide.

**If I could ship only three things:** #1 (Dixon-Coles scorelines) + #2 (exp-link λ + home) as one afternoon's quick win, the **tiered-points + RPS** scoring for the mini-game, and the **calibration harness** to prove it. Those three convert "plausible-looking random goals" into *measurably realistic* football, and turn the palpites game from a toy into a fair, gameable-only-by-being-good competition — all within static Pages + Supabase, zero new hosting.
